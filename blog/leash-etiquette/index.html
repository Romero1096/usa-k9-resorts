Leash Etiquette: Why It Matters More Than You Think

September 15, 2026USA K9 Resorts

Leash etiquette is one of the first things we work on with every dog that comes through our facility — and for good reason. A dog that pulls, lunges, or wraps around your legs isn't just frustrating, it's a genuine safety risk for you, your dog, and anyone nearby.

The root cause is almost always the same: the dog has learned that pulling gets them where they want to go. Every time they lunge toward a squirrel and you follow, they get rewarded. The fix isn't about strength — it's about communication and consistency.

We start by teaching the dog that a loose leash is the only way forward. The moment tension appears, we stop. We wait. We redirect. Over time, the dog figures out that calm, loose-leash walking is the behavior that moves the walk along. It takes patience, but it works with every breed and every age.

A few things that help: always start your walk with a calm exit from the house (no excited rushing out the door), use a properly fitted collar or harness, and keep sessions short at first — 10 to 15 minutes of focused practice beats a 45-minute battle. If your dog is reactive to other dogs or people, work in low-distraction environments first and build up gradually.
